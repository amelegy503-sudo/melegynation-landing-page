Most people don't lack effort. They lack structure.

If you've been consistent and still stuck, it's rarely a discipline problem. It's a planning problem.

  • Random workoutsEvery session is a new idea. None of them build on the last.
  • A new program every three weeksNothing runs long enough to work, so nothing gets a fair test.
  • Training hard, going nowhereEffort was never the missing ingredient. Direction is.
  • Dieting with no structureMonday's plan doesn't survive Thursday.
  • Ignoring the limitationA tight hip or a cranky shoulder is information. Training around it blindly isn't a strategy.
  • Knowing the exercises, not the systemYou know what a row is. You don't know how it fits into a week, a month, a block.
  • Not knowing what to do when something hurtsPush through, modify or stop? Guessing is the worst of the three.

Calisthenics is training, not a circuit.

Bodyweight training done properly is a legitimate way to build strength, control and skill. It's also how you earn a handstand without wrecking your wrists.

  • Pull-ups and dips

    Upper-body strength built through progressions you can measure, from your first rep to weighted work.

  • Push-ups

    Not just a warm-up. Easier variations to build from, harder ones when you've earned them.

  • Handstands and skills

    Skill work gets structured practice and progressions, plus the shoulder and wrist preparation it needs.

  • Body control

    Strength you can actually use: position, tension and control through the full range.

  • Mobility

    The range of motion calisthenics demands, built alongside the strength.

  • Relative strength

    Getting stronger for your bodyweight. It's why a simple-looking pull-up is rarely simple.

Calisthenics can be the main focus of your program or one part of a wider plan that includes weights.

Is this suitable for beginners?

Yes. Beginners get a simpler structure and more focus on technique and fundamentals. Experienced lifters get progression that doesn't reset every few weeks. Either way, you won't be treated like someone you're not.

Do I need a gym?

No, but it changes the plan. Full gym, home setup, minimal equipment or bodyweight and calisthenics all work. Tell me what you have in the application and I'll build around it.

Can you work around injuries or limitations?

Within the scope of coaching, yes: injury-aware programming, corrective exercise and exercise-based support for returning to training.

I'm not a doctor or physiotherapist. I don't diagnose or treat injuries. If you have a diagnosed injury or medical condition, work alongside the right healthcare professional where necessary, and get cleared before we train around it.

Can I do calisthenics only?

Yes. Calisthenics can be the whole focus of your program or sit alongside weights. Tell me your current level and the skills you want in the application.

Do you provide nutrition plans?

Yes. Nutrition guidance is part of the coaching and is adjusted as your progress changes. It's coaching guidance, not medical or dietitian advice. If you have a medical condition, check with your doctor first.
